## Fact check

Acted as a skeptical historian and checked every significant claim against web search:
- **Internal date contradiction (fixed).** The draft opened "This is the year thirteen ten" but Chapter
  5 later says the household is "a generation removed from the hardest of those famine years" —
  referring to the Great Famine of 1315-1317, which hadn't happened yet in 1310. Moved the story's year
  to "thirteen forty" (1340), which sits a clean generation after the famine and still fits inside the
  Little Ice Age cooling window (onset ~1300, confirmed by search) and the "warm medieval period" the
  grandparents would remember.
- **Couvre-feu / curfew and William the Conqueror.** Verified: the word and the fire-covering practice
  are real, but the popular "William the Conqueror invented it" story is itself a simplification — the
  practice likely predates the Conquest and William's own motives were partly political. The draft
  already hedges with "is often traced back to," which matches how this is usually presented; no
  verifiable claim is stated as hard fact, so left as is.
- **Segmented sleep (Roger Ekirch / "first sleep" and "second sleep").** Verified Ekirch's research and
  Craig Koslofsky's supporting work are real and accurately described. Also confirmed a genuine, more
  recent historians' debate (a 2023 Medical History reconsideration) questioning how universal the
  pattern really was. The draft already includes this nuance ("Not every historian accepts every detail
  of this picture, and the debate continues even now") — confirmed accurate, no change needed.
- **Little Ice Age timing.** "Around the turn into the thirteen hundreds" matches the broadly accepted
  ~1300 (sometimes 1257-1300) onset date found across sources. No change.
- **Great Famine of 1315-1317.** Dates, two-year span, and "rains" cause confirmed accurate. No change.
- **Chilblains remedy (egg, wine, fennel root).** Confirmed as a real historical remedy from Bald's
  Leechbook (a 10th-century Anglo-Saxon medical text) rather than invented; plausible as inherited
  household lore by the 1340s setting. Left as "one old household remedy" (already appropriately vague
  about its exact age) — no change.
- **Cat fur among "sheep, rabbit, or sometimes cat."** Confirmed: lamb, rabbit/coney, and cat were
  documented "lowly" furs worn by common people under sumptuary law, while cat skins specifically came
  from strays. Accurate as written. No change.
- **Plough Monday church customs (plough kept in church, plough-light candle).** Confirmed real, but
  best documented from the 15th century — a hundred years or so after this video's setting. Softened
  "a plough is kept" to "a plough is sometimes kept" to avoid overstating how established the custom
  was this early; the surrounding hedges ("in some parishes," "sometimes") already carried most of the
  weight.
- **Canonical hours, Candlemas weather lore, Martinmas pig-slaughter, manor week-work/manor court,
  peasant diet (pottage, maslin/dredge bread, home-brewed ale), wassailing/mumming/boar's head feast.**
  All checked against at least two sources each and confirmed as standard, well-supported medieval
  English practice; no changes needed.

  Couvre-feu, Rushlight, Little Ice Age, Great Famine of 1315-1317, Chilblains, Canonical hours, Plough
